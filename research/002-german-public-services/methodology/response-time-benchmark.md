# Response-Time Benchmark Methodology

## Objective

Measure and compare administrative responsiveness across Jobcenter offices,
departments and communication channels.

The benchmark measures service responsiveness and accessibility.

It does NOT directly measure political loyalty, friendliness, corruption,
intent or discrimination.

## Core metrics

For each request record:

- institution
- department / organisational unit
- district
- communication channel
- request category
- date/time sent
- automatic acknowledgement time
- first human response time
- first substantive response time
- final resolution time
- number of transfers / referrals
- number of clarification requests
- whether all questions were answered
- whether legal basis was provided
- whether source documents were provided
- language of response
- interpreter / language-support availability
- outcome

## Time measurements

### T_ACK
Time from sending to automatic acknowledgement.

### T_FIRST
Time from sending to first human response.

### T_SUBSTANTIVE
Time from sending to first response that materially addresses the request.

### T_RESOLUTION
Time from sending to final resolution.

## Quality dimensions

Response speed must be separated from response quality.

Suggested quality fields:

- completeness
- specificity
- legal grounding
- source transparency
- actionability
- accessibility
- consistency

## Comparability rules

Comparisons should account for:

- working days
- weekends
- public holidays
- channel used
- request complexity
- institution type
- whether the request was routed correctly
- whether clarification was required

Whenever possible, use identical or functionally equivalent requests.

## Interpretation

A fast response is not necessarily a good response.

A slower but complete and legally grounded answer may provide higher service
quality than a rapid template response.

Observed differences should initially be classified as administrative
responsiveness differences.

Systematic discrimination, corruption or intentional obstruction require
additional evidence.

## Research output

Possible derived metrics:

- median response time by institution
- median substantive-response time
- acknowledgement rate
- full-answer rate
- legal-citation rate
- referral rate
- no-response rate
- language-support rate
- resolution rate
